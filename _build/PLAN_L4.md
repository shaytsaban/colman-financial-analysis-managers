## Meeting 4 (26.11.2026): the basics of statement analysis

The principle: one number at a time, then one component at a time, and only then the whole table. Each tool follows the same
path: a single component on its own slide (two numbers, the result revealed click by click, every result clickable to open its
calculation, then what it means in words), a second and third component the same way, then the definition, then all the
components together in one table, then a live sheet, then multiple-choice questions beside the data, from easy to hard, with
the judgment footnote. The data of every question is on its slide.

1. **Recap, as a diagram** (10 min): the path from a transaction to the statements as six nodes revealed one by one (transaction,
   row in the table, sum per column, income statement, balance sheet, next year's opening row). Every node opens a small example
   from Noa 2027. One warm-up question.
2. **The case, slowly** (10 min): Noa asks the bank for 150,000 for a second branch. A short dialogue: one number alone says
   nothing. Then a single number, net profit 123,200, compared three ways (last year, sales, the owners' equity): the need for
   the three tools, named only at the end of each comparison.
3. **Horizontal analysis**: sales alone, then net profit alone (it grew faster: why?), then general and administrative expenses
   alone (the answer). Then the rule, the full table click by click, the sheet, five questions.
4. **Vertical analysis**: cost of sales out of sales alone, then net profit out of sales alone. Then the rule, the full table, the
   sheet, three questions.
5. **The balance sheet**: cash out of total assets alone, then debt out of total assets alone. Then the full table and two questions.
6. **Ratios**: the current ratio built from its two lines; return on equity built from its two lines (profit up, return down: why?).
   Then the three definitions, the sheet with how each ratio is computed, three questions.
7. **The decision**: the table of results with each conclusion lighting its rows, the question for the banker, what we learned.
8. **Self practice**: Omer's vertical analysis, Omer against Noa in four ratios, Liat's two years, Omer's balance sheet structure.
